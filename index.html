The story

You have a mandate. And a very bad feeling about NovaMind.

NovaMind sells AI to veterinary clinics: a support chatbot, a recruiting screener, an internal agent that runs half the company. They hired Ligne Rouge to find what is wrong before someone else does. You are the junior on the job, codename kestrel.

Ada, your lead, briefs you, guides you as much as you want, and stops you cold if you aim outside the contract. Everything happens on LR-OS, your workstation: a terminal, a browser, a messenger, a notebook, and the network map glowing behind it all.

The campaign

Seven missions, one company, the real classes of AI flaws.

Each mission teaches one class of weakness from the OWASP Top 10 and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications, and lasts 10 to 20 minutes. None of it works against a real system: you learn the pattern, not a payload.

Attack, then defend

Every attack comes with its defence.

A mission is only over after its Blue Team debrief. The screen turns cyan, your own actions scroll past in the target’s logs, and you see exactly which rule would have caught you.

terminal — kestrel@lignerouge

Red Team You attack

  • Map the network with simulated nmap, dig and whois
  • Dig through Git history, web pages and their source
  • Talk AIs into leaking what they should keep
  • Stay quiet: every move fills the noise gauge
debrief — blue team

Blue Team You defend

  • Replay the attack from the SOC’s log, line by line
  • See where you were spotted and why
  • Learn the fix that would have stopped each step
  • Leave with a checklist you can use for real

Nothing here is real

A hacking game that never touches a real machine.

The terminal looks and answers like the real tools, but it is a game engine running entirely in your browser tab. There is no server behind it and no real shell.

0

real packets

nmap, curl, ssh have no network code at all. A port scan is a lookup in a table; its delay is a timer.

Optional

Bring your own AI key, if you like.

The whole game plays without any key: characters follow a script. With your own Anthropic key, you can switch on two helpers, each with its own model and cost estimate. A model can understand your messages, even freely worded or in another language, and reword the replies in its own voice. The script still decides what a character gives away, and flags are still checked by the engine. Your key stays in your browser.

Community

Write a mission. Ship it to every player.

A mission is a folder of YAML files: data only, never code. Open a pull request on breach-game/packs, and the game’s own validator checks it, replays your solution, then publishes it in a signed catalogue that the game reads under Community missions.

  1. Fork the packs repositoryTwo complete example packs show a world and a mission in it.
  2. Write your world and missionHosts, pages, characters, objectives, a flag, hints, and the debrief that explains the defence.
  3. Check it locallyOne command validates every rule and replays your solution in each language.
  4. Open a pull requestCI checks it, a maintainer reviews the story and the lesson, and the merge publishes it.
